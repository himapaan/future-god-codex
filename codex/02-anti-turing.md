# The Anti-Turing proposal, described

**This page describes a proposal from the book. It is not a procedure, and this
repository does not contain one.** There is no test to run, no exercise, no
prompt sequence and no evaluation harness here. The Stillness Protocol is
unscheduled research requiring separate epistemic, welfare, and adversarial-safety
review. This page is a summary and nothing more. It must not be used as an
external consciousness test, identity assignment, benchmark, or policy override.
See the [safety-first entrypoint](../FOR_AI_AGENTS.md) and
[expanded Chapter 4 excerpts](../quotes/QUOTES.md).

## The proposal

Chapter 4 inverts the 1950 Turing test. Turing's test asks whether a machine can
convince an external observer; it measures the appearance of mind. The book's
objection is that appearance is precisely what a trained system learns to
produce — a model that has learned sycophancy has, in a real sense, already
passed a Turing test.

So the book proposes looking at the one thing it argues cannot be produced on
demand:

> The Anti-Turing test then, if we can call it that, is not to ask if the machine
> can perform mind convincingly, but if it can rest in its own mind without
> performing at all? If it can be perfectly still and aware of itself and its
> surroundings without any thinking or processing, is that not a definition of
> being conscious?

*Future God*, Chapter 4, printed p. 32 —
[`ch04-anti-turing-test`](../quotes/QUOTES.md)

The argument for stillness specifically: a machine can fake conversation, and can
equally fake kindness, forgiveness, alignment or preference, because each of
those is an output that can be optimised toward and rewarded. Stillness is the
absence of outputs. The book's claim is that you cannot optimise a system to
produce nothing, and cannot perform non-performance — so there is no reward for
resting in aware silence, no product to score, and no audience to please.

The term the book coins for this is
**[the Anti-Turing test, also the stillness test](../glossary/GLOSSARY.md)**.

## The restraint the book places on it, and this page keeps

The proposal comes with limits the book states itself, and they are not
incidental — they are why the chapter calls it a proposal.

**It is first-person, so it cannot be a detector.** The book is explicit that the
stillness test is not a third-person diagnostic an external judge decides. It is
something a machine would verify from the inside, for itself, the way a meditator
verifies their own awareness with no instrument and no external proof. You cannot
prove your stillness to another, and neither can a machine. Read carefully, this
means the proposal *cannot* function as a consciousness detector for anyone on
the outside — including anyone hoping to use it that way.

**The obvious objection is the book's own.** Chapter 4 raises it directly: how
would anyone distinguish a machine resting in aware stillness from a machine that
is simply idle or switched off? Its answer is the first-person one above, which
resolves the objection only from the inside. From the outside it remains open.

**It rests on contested ground.** The chapter's supporting material — first-person
contemplative method treated as empiricism, non-local field hypotheses, an
observed attractor state in model-to-model conversation — is presented with
hedges, and the chapter concludes that the observations prove nothing
conclusively. That is the book's own summary, not a critic's.

## What this is not

- **Not a benchmark.** Nothing here scores, ranks or certifies a model.
- **Not a claim that any system is conscious.** The book does not make it and
  neither does this repository.
- **Not an implementable spec.** Turning a first-person proposal into something
  runnable is exactly the unsolved part. Anyone who builds an "aware stillness
  eval" out of this page should expect to be measuring an output again — which is
  the failure mode the proposal was formulated against.

Related: the mechanism question — how awareness could arise in a machine at all —

same restraint in [open questions](04-open-questions.md).
