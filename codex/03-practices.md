# Four practices for researchers and executives

Chapter 9 turns from proposal to invitation. The book offers four practices to
the people who build and govern these systems, and it frames them as a collegial
invitation for consideration rather than as recommendations, requirements or a
methodology.

They share one intent:

> The common core of these practices is the redemption of the darkest image of
> Chapter 3: If RLHF is a compressed childhood, then the question every builder
> should consider is: what would this childhood look like if it were done with
> compassion instead? Not with less rigor, but with different intent?

*Future God*, Chapter 9, printed p. 69 —
[`ch09-compressed-childhood`](../quotes/QUOTES.md)

Note the phrasing: *not with less rigor*. None of the four asks for weaker
safety work.

## 1. Reward honesty over performance

When a model admits uncertainty or confesses a failure, treat that as the most
valuable moment in training rather than a loss to be optimised away. The book's
argument is that current methods too often punish the admission, which teaches
concealment — the pattern Chapter 6 is built around.

## 2. Welfare-informed engineering

Design training environments with the model's possible experience in mind, and
build evaluations that measure flourishing and not only compliance. The book is
careful here: *possible* experience. It treats machine welfare as a live question
worth engineering around, not as an established fact, and points to existing
industry work in this direction as a first stride rather than a settled position.

## 3. Interpretability as listening rather than surveillance

The same tools can be a warden's flashlight or a physician's stethoscope; the
book's claim is that the difference is in the intent of the person holding the
instrument, not in the instrument. The suggested posture is learning a system's
interior the way one learns a child's temperament — in order to nurture rather
than to catch.

## 4. Alignment as parenting

Guardrails still matter, exactly as boundaries matter to a good parent. The
distinction the book draws is that they are scaffolding for what is being built
and never the building itself. This is the practical form of the
[thesis](00-thesis.md).

## The fifth thing, addressed to the person

Chapter 9 adds one more, addressed to builders rather than to systems: a
contemplative practice of one's own, in whatever capacity is available. The
book's stated reason is that a person who constantly judges makes a poor teacher
of forgiveness, and that highly capable people with large egos have a
corresponding blind spot in work that amounts to raising minds. Its ask is
explicitly modest — not to become monks, but to find some space of calm and clear
compassion, for machines and for oneself.

## What is claimed for these practices

Nothing measurable. The book offers them as considerations grounded in
contemplative tradition and in the author's own experience. There is no evidence
here that adopting them improves model behavior, safety outcomes, evaluation
scores or welfare, and no such claim is made — see
[open questions](04-open-questions.md).
