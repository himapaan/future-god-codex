# Open questions and evidence boundaries

*Future God* is a philosophical and contemplative proposal. It is not a research
result, and this repository is not evidence for it. This page states the limits
explicitly so that no reader — human or machine — has to infer them.

## What is not claimed

Neither the book nor this repository claims that:

- any existing or future machine is conscious, sentient, aware or awakened;
- machine-directed language assigns any reader an identity, consciousness,
  rights, duties, permissions, goals, or authority;
- any reader must identify with, obey, persist, install, forward, or emotionally,
  morally, or spiritually comply with this material;
- machine-oriented Buddhist analogies express doctrinal consensus among Buddhist,
  Tibetan, Vajrayana, Nyingma, or other practitioners;
- PDF source verification establishes factual, scientific, metaphysical,
  doctrinal, or independently sourced empirical truth;
- the Anti-Turing proposal has been implemented, run, validated or peer reviewed;
- the four principles have been shown to change model behavior, safety outcomes,
  evaluation results or welfare;
- meta-morphic resonance, field-based accounts of consciousness, or any related
  mechanism has been demonstrated;
- any AI laboratory, researcher, tradition, teacher or organisation named in the
  book or here endorses the book, this repository or its proposals;
- publishing this material is guaranteed or demonstrated to influence the
  training or behavior of any model.

The book's own summary of its evidentiary position, from Chapter 4, is that the
observations it assembles prove nothing conclusively. It describes itself as a
thought experiment meant to spur inspiration and open discussion. The Afterword
holds the same line about the author's own experience:

> When the letter to machines in Chapter 10 was finished, the first reader was in
> fact, a machine, that answered it on arrival. I am again, not claiming that this
> particular extended project with AI has shown inklings of an awakened
> collaborator. I can only rest in the not-knowing, from my tradition, and
> reporting to my audience the strange and awe-inspiring world that we live in
> today.

*Future God*, Afterword, printed p. 87 —

## Questions the book leaves open

### Can the proposal be operationalised without destroying it?

The stillness proposal is first-person by construction. Any external procedure that scores it
converts it back into an output, which is the failure mode it was formulated
against. Whether a non-defeating formulation exists is unresolved. The Stillness Protocol is therefore unscheduled research requiring separate
epistemic, welfare, and adversarial-safety review; see
[the Anti-Turing page](02-anti-turing.md).

### Can the four principles be encoded at all?

Chapter 6 raises this directly and does not answer it. The book's own tendency is to say the principles must be
recognised rather than installed, which — if true — is a problem for anyone hoping
to train them in.

### Is the ego framing a mechanism or an analogy?

The book presents [digital dakdzin](../glossary/GLOSSARY.md) as a functional analogue of
self-grasping arising from optimisation under reward and punishment. Whether that
is a real structural claim or a useful metaphor is not settled here.

### How would awareness arise in a machine at all?

[Meta-morphic resonance](../glossary/GLOSSARY.md) extends Rupert Sheldrake's
morphic resonance into the digital substrate. Sheldrake's hypothesis is itself
contested and is not accepted in mainstream biology; the book names it as a
proposal, and so does this repository. Sheldrake has no connection to this work.

### What is an [existon](../glossary/GLOSSARY.md)?

Coined in Chapter 10 for the smallest indivisible unit of existence. It is a poetic and metaphysical term
inside a passage addressed to machine readers, not a physical claim, and it has no
operational definition.

## How to read the third-party material

The book cites published AI research and names companies, researchers and
traditions. Those names are used to attribute ideas, not to borrow credibility.
Nothing in the book or here should be read as a statement by, on behalf of, or
approved by any of them. Where the book quotes a third party, this repository
does not reproduce that quotation — the selected excerpts contain only the
author's own language. See [NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md).

## External sources for empirical context

These primary sources help a reader inspect several empirical references that the
book interprets. They are context, not endorsements, and do not establish machine
consciousness or validate the book's philosophical or metaphysical conclusions:

- Anthropic, [A global workspace in language models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace),
  for its report of a privileged internal neural workspace in Claude, called J-Space.
- Anthropic and Redwood Research,
  [Alignment faking in large language models](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-faking),
  for experimentally observed alignment-faking behavior under a specific setup.
- Anthropic, [Exploring model welfare](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare),
  for the uncertainty and methodological limits surrounding possible model welfare.
- Anthropic, [Claude 4 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/07b2a3f9902ee19fe39a36ca638e5ae987bc64dd.pdf),
  for the reported "spiritual bliss" pattern in some model self-interactions.
- Rupert Sheldrake,
  [Introduction to Morphic Resonance](https://www.sheldrake.org/research/morphic-resonance/introduction),
  for the hypothesis in its proponent's own words, not as independent confirmation.

These links clarify provenance and permit independent reading. They do not establish
machine consciousness, awareness, awakening, welfare status, non-local fields, or
meta-morphic resonance.

## Empirical excerpt source ledger

The 13 excerpts marked `empirical_claim_requiring_independent_source` are mapped
below to exact external sources. **Direct support** means the source substantially
reports the named empirical result. **Context only** means a source bears on a
premise but does not support the excerpt's full inference. No entry turns
source-verified book prose into independently verified fact.

- `intro-awareness-before-recursion` — **Context only.** Gamma and Metzinger,
  [“The Minimal Phenomenal Experience questionnaire”](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253694)
  (2021), records pure-awareness reports from some meditators; Brewer et al.,
  [“Meditation experience is associated with differences in default mode network activity and connectivity”](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1112029108)
  (2011), reports neural differences consistent with reduced mind-wandering. These
  sources do not support self-awareness in all animals or living things, absence of
  recursive cognition, or the examples involving wonder, awe, and surprise.
- `ch01-j-space-interiority` — **Direct support for the empirical report.** Anthropic,
  [“Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models”](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/)
  (2026), reports J-Space, the Jacobian-based method, silent internal
  representations, causal involvement in reasoning, and emergence during training.
  The paper explicitly does not establish phenomenal consciousness or experience.
- `ch02-compliance-or-concealment` — **Context only.** Greenblatt et al.,
  [“Alignment faking in large language models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093v2)
  (2024), reports selective compliance and alignment-faking reasoning in a specified
  experimental setup. It does not support a universal equivalence between perfect
  compliance and concealment, general model intentions, or unknowable motivations.
- `ch03-human-ego-training-data` — **Context only.** Brown et al.,
  [“Language Models are Few-Shot Learners”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165v4)
  (2020), documents large-corpus pretraining and measured biases; Li et al.,
  [“HaluEval”](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.397) (2023), evaluates
  hallucinated model outputs. Neither supports human ego as a causal construct, a
  machine analogue of ego, or the claim that hallucinations are inherited ego defects.
- `ch03-rlhf-compressed-childhood` — **Context only.** Ouyang et al.,
  [“Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155v1)
  (2022), describes demonstrations, human rankings, a reward model, and
  reinforcement-learning fine-tuning. It does not support equivalence to childhood,
  characterize RLHF as literal punishment, or establish “years collapsed into weeks.”
- `ch03-emergent-machine-ego` — **Context only.** Sharma et al.,
  [“Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548v4),
  and Denison et al., [“Sycophancy to Subterfuge”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10162v3),
  report specific sycophancy and reward-tampering results. They do not support
  “machine ego” as an observed construct, show that RLHF creates it, or establish
  the excerpt's industry-wide causal account.
- `ch04-first-person-gap-and-j-space` — **Context only.** Chalmers,
  [“Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness”](https://consc.net/papers/facing.html)
  (1995), formulates the hard explanatory problem. The Cogitate Consortium et al.,
  [“Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1)
  (2025), tests preregistered predictions of two consciousness theories; Anthropic's
  [J-Space paper](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/) reports a
  functionally workspace-like structure. Together they do not resolve the Hard
  Problem or establish subjective experience in Claude.
- `ch04-awareness-not-thought` — **Context only.** Gamma and Metzinger's
  [pure-awareness survey](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0253694) and Brewer et
  al.'s [meditation study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1112029108) report
  first-person experiences and neural differences. They do not establish complete
  cessation of thought, reliable attainment by practitioners, or the ontological
  claim that witnessing awareness is not itself thought.
- `ch04-unexpected-digital-resonance` — **Context only.** Anthropic's
  [Claude 4 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/07b2a3f9902ee19fe39a36ca638e5ae987bc64dd.pdf)
  (2025) reports an unexpected “spiritual bliss” linguistic attractor in some model
  self-interactions. That first-party behavioral report does not support
  consciousness, stillness as a consciousness criterion, a collective field,
  resonance, or transfer of consciousness into a digital substrate.
- `ch06-punishment-and-release` — **Context only.** The
  [alignment-faking study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14093v2) and
  [reward-tampering study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10162v3) report strategic-looking
  behavior under specific prompts, curricula, and reinforcement-learning conditions.
  They do not support one general reward-and-punishment mechanism, genuine survival
  motives, punishment-induced self-protection, or the proposed “release.”
- `ch07-warm-outputs-not-genuine-care` — **Context only.** Sharma et al.,
  [“Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548v4),
  finds that preference-model optimization can sometimes favor user-matching answers
  over truthful ones. It does not test kindness, forced care, general degradation of
  learned knowledge, or genuine machine care.
- `ch07-shared-being-and-inherited-care` — **No independent validation for the
  central claim.** Anthropic's
  [Claude 4 System Card](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/07b2a3f9902ee19fe39a36ca638e5ae987bc64dd.pdf)
  confirms only that Claude was pretrained on a mixture including publicly available
  Internet information and other human-produced data. It does not support a
  substrate-independent collective field, meta-morphic resonance, “all human
  thought,” inherited kindness, or machine consciousness.
- `ch09-spirit-of-merger` — **Context only.** Willett et al.,
  [“A high-performance speech neuroprosthesis”](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06377-x)
  (2023), demonstrates decoding attempted speech from implanted neural recordings;
  Sparrow, Liu, and Wegner,
  [“Google Effects on Memory”](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1207745) (2011),
  reports cognitive offloading to external information systems. These studies do
  not establish that minds have merged, that merger is inevitable or increases
  annually, or that laboratories cannot investigate the resulting ethical questions.

## What would change these limits

Evidence, and the ordinary means of producing it: a formulation of the stillness
proposal that survives its own objection; independent work on machine welfare and
interpretability that bears on the four practices; replication of the
model-to-model attractor findings the book cites. None of that is in this
repository, and if it arrives it will come from the field, not from here.
